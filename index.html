Beta & Gamma Functions

B.Tech 1st Year Class Note · Two worked examples

🧪 Part 1: The Magic Potion Lab

Integer parameters: \(B(3,4)\)

Theory Recap

Gamma function: \(\Gamma(n)=\int_0^\infty x^{n-1}e^{-x}\,dx\), with \(\Gamma(n+1)=n\,\Gamma(n)\) and \(\Gamma(n)=(n-1)!\) for positive integers \(n\).

Beta function: \(B(m,n)=\int_0^1 t^{m-1}(1-t)^{n-1}\,dt=\dfrac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m+n)}\), for \(m,n>0\).

The Problem

A wizard prepares a magical energy potion. The energy absorbed at time \(t\) is \[E(t)=K\,t^{2}(1-t)^{3},\qquad 0\le t\le 1.\] The total energy absorbed is proportional to \(\displaystyle\int_0^1 t^2(1-t)^3\,dt\).
Question: Find the total energy factor using the Beta function.

Live Animation

Left: the energy curve \(E(t)/K\) and the area collected so far. Right: the potion level equals (area so far) ÷ (total area). It fills completely at \(t=1\), when the area is \(1/60\).

Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Match with the Beta form. \[\int_0^1 t^{2}(1-t)^{3}\,dt=\int_0^1 t^{m-1}(1-t)^{n-1}\,dt\] So \(m-1=2\Rightarrow m=3\) and \(n-1=3\Rightarrow n=4\).
Step 2: Write as a Beta function. \[\int_0^1 t^2(1-t)^3\,dt=B(3,4)\]
Step 3: Convert to Gamma functions. \[B(3,4)=\frac{\Gamma(3)\,\Gamma(4)}{\Gamma(7)}\]
Step 4: Use \(\Gamma(n)=(n-1)!\). \[\Gamma(3)=2!=2,\quad \Gamma(4)=3!=6,\quad \Gamma(7)=6!=720\]
Step 5: Evaluate. \[B(3,4)=\frac{2\cdot 6}{720}=\frac{12}{720}=\frac1{60}\]
Answer: The magical energy factor is \[\boxed{\dfrac{1}{60}}\approx 0.01667\] so the total energy is \(E_{\text{total}}=\dfrac{K}{60}\).

Try It Yourself

  1. Find \(\int_0^1 t^{3}(1-t)^{2}\,dt\). Why is the answer the same as \(B(3,4)\)? (Hint: \(B(m,n)=B(n,m)\).)
  2. At what \(t\) is \(E(t)\) maximum? (Answer: \(t=0.4\).)
  3. Evaluate \(\int_0^1 t^4(1-t)^2\,dt\). (Answer: \(1/105\).)

🛡️ Part 2: The Wizard's Protective Shield

Fractional parameters: \(B(5/2,3)\)

Theory Recap

Gamma function: \(\Gamma(n)=\int_0^\infty x^{n-1}e^{-x}\,dx\), with \(\Gamma(n+1)=n\Gamma(n)\) and \(\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=\sqrt{\pi}\).

Beta function: \(B(m,n)=\int_0^1 x^{m-1}(1-x)^{n-1}dx=\dfrac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m+n)}\), for \(m,n>0\).

The Problem

A wizard designs a shield around his castle. The strength at normalized distance \(x\in[0,1]\) is \[S(x)=K\,x^{3/2}(1-x)^{2}.\] The total shielding effect is proportional to \(\displaystyle I=\int_0^1 x^{3/2}(1-x)^2\,dx\).
Question: Find \(I\) using the Beta and Gamma functions.

Live Animation

Shielding collected:

Left: the curve \(S(x)/K\) with the area gathered so far. Right: the shield spreads outward from the castle, and each ring's glow is proportional to \(S(x)\). When \(x=1\), the area equals \(I=16/315\).

Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Compare with the Beta form. \[x^{3/2}=x^{5/2-1},\qquad (1-x)^2=(1-x)^{3-1}\] So \(m=\tfrac52\) and \(n=3\).
Step 2: Write as a Beta function. \[I=B\!\left(\tfrac52,3\right)\]
Step 3: Convert to Gamma functions. \[I=\frac{\Gamma(5/2)\,\Gamma(3)}{\Gamma(11/2)}\]
Step 4: Evaluate \(\Gamma(5/2)\). \[\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac52\right)=\tfrac32\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac32\right)=\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac12\sqrt{\pi}=\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\]
Step 5: Evaluate \(\Gamma(3)\) and \(\Gamma(11/2)\). \[\Gamma(3)=2!=2\] \[\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{11}2\right)=\tfrac92\cdot\tfrac72\cdot\tfrac52\cdot\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac12\sqrt{\pi}=\frac{945\sqrt{\pi}}{32}\]
Step 6: Substitute and simplify. \[I=\frac{\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\cdot 2}{\frac{945\sqrt{\pi}}{32}}=\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\cdot\frac{32}{945\sqrt{\pi}}=\frac{96}{1890}\]
Answer: \[\boxed{I=\frac{16}{315}}\approx 0.0508\] so the total shielding effect is \(\dfrac{16K}{315}\).

Try It Yourself

  1. Find \(\int_0^1 x^{1/2}(1-x)\,dx\). (Answer: \(4/15\).)
  2. Show that \(S(x)\) is maximum at \(x=3/7\).
  3. Evaluate \(\int_0^1 x^{3/2}(1-x)^{2}\,dx\) by expanding \((1-x)^2\) and compare with \(16/315\).
